About this name generator

Fantasy & D&D names

Our fantasy name generator draws on naming conventions from high fantasy literature and tabletop RPG traditions. Elven names are designed to feel melodic and ancient, drawing on the phonetic patterns established by Tolkien and expanded by decades of the genre. Dwarven names carry characteristic strength and brevity. Fae names reflect the liminal, natural quality associated with that tradition.

D&D players will find class-specific name suggestions calibrated to each class archetype — a Wizard's name carries different phonetic weight than a Fighter's. Tone and class are baked in to help you find a name that fits your character concept, not just the setting.

Baby names by origin and meaning

Each baby name in our database includes its etymology and meaning — because the most memorable names carry a story. We cover Celtic, Scandinavian, Latin, Greek, Hebrew, Japanese and Arabic origins, reflecting the rich diversity of naming traditions parents draw on today. Unlike lists that surface the same hundred popular names, we've curated options that are distinctive without being invented — classic but not overused.

Business & brand names

A good business name is short, distinctive, and available as a domain. Our business name generator offers five distinct style registers — from clean modern coinages to professional partnership-style names. Use these as creative inspiration, then check domain availability for your favourites. The business category is particularly useful for early-stage founders who need naming options before committing to brand development.

Team, pet, sci-fi and villain names

Our team name generator covers four tonal registers — fierce, funny, clever and mysterious — making it equally useful for competitive sports teams, quiz nights, corporate team-building events, and online gaming squads. Pet names range from cute and regal to mythological, covering dogs, cats, rabbits, birds and small animals. The sci-fi and villain categories are designed for fiction writers, game masters, and creative projects needing distinctive character names with the right atmospheric weight.

Privacy and data

All name generation happens entirely in your browser. No data is transmitted to any server, no names are recorded, and no account is required. You can generate names for any purpose without any privacy concern.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use these names commercially?

Yes. All names generated by this tool are free to use for any purpose — personal projects, published fiction, games, business names, and more. Names themselves are not subject to copyright. If a generated name happens to match an existing trademark, check clearance before commercial use.

How are the names generated?

Names are drawn from hand-curated lists built around each category's conventions — phonetic patterns for fantasy races, etymological research for baby names, and branding principles for business names. The randomisation happens entirely in your browser; no names are sent to or stored on any server.

How many names can I generate?

Unlimited. Click Generate as many times as you like. Each click draws a fresh random selection from the curated pool for your chosen category and filters. There is no usage limit or sign-up required.

How do I save names I like?

Click any name tile to save it to your Saved bar at the top of the page. Click a saved name to remove it. Double-click any name tile to copy it directly to your clipboard. The Copy All button copies every currently displayed name at once.

What origins are covered for baby names?

The baby names category covers English, Celtic, Scandinavian, Latin, Greek, Hebrew, Japanese and Arabic origins, with gender filters for feminine, masculine and unisex names. Each name includes its etymology and meaning. We update the database regularly based on user feedback, particularly to expand underrepresented cultural origins.

Can I suggest names to add?

Yes — use the contact link in the footer. We regularly update each category's name pool based on user feedback, particularly for underrepresented cultural origins in the baby names section and new game systems in the D&D category.
